# Code of conduct

texshelf is a small project, and most people who show up here are students
and researchers sharing templates they made in their spare time. Treat them
the way you would treat a classmate who lent you their notes.

## What we expect

- Be patient with questions, including ones the docs already answer.
- Criticise the template or the code, and leave the person out of it.
- Give credit. If your template builds on someone else's, say so and keep
  their license.
- Write in English or Spanish. If you are more comfortable in another
  language, write in it and add a machine translation.

## What is not acceptable

- Insults, harassment, or jokes at the expense of someone's identity,
  background or level of experience.
- Publishing someone's private information.
- Submitting a template you do not have the right to share, or removing its
  author's name.
- Continuing to contact someone who has asked you to stop.

## Where this applies

Issues, pull requests, discussions and reviews in every repository under
[github.com/texshelf](https://github.com/texshelf), and any other place where
you are speaking for the project.

## Reporting

Write to hello@jjsm.science. Juan José Sánchez Medina reads that address and
will reply within a week. Reports stay private. If the report is about him,
use GitHub's own
[abuse report form](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam)
instead.

A maintainer may edit or remove comments, close threads, or block an account
from the organization. A first problem usually gets a private note. Repeated
or serious ones get a block.
